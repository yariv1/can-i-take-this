Duty-Free Alcohol Allowance by Country (2026): Litres of Wine, Beer & Spirits You Can Bring

Customs & Money · Updated 2026 · 9 min read

The alcohol allowance is set by the country you are arriving in, and it runs from nothing at all in Saudi Arabia and the Maldives to 42 litres of beer in the UK. The most common cap is a single litre of spirits — but the same bag of bottles that is fine in one country is over the limit in the next.

Below is the 2026 alcohol allowance for 43 destinations, taken from each country’s own customs authority: litres or bottles of spirits, wine and beer, plus the minimum age. After the table: how the limits are counted, whether spirits, wine and beer stack, the countries that ban or restrict alcohol, and what happens when you go over.

A standard wine bottle is 750 ml. A six-bottle case is 4.5 litres, so it is over the EU limit of 4 litres of still wine but fine in Mexico (6 litres of wine) or the UK (18 litres). Count in litres, not bottles, unless the country counts in bottles.

Litres, bottles or alcohol units: how countries count

Most customs authorities count litres. A few count bottles or units, and that changes what you can buy.

New Zealand counts bottles: 3 bottles of spirits, up to 1.125 litres each — or 4.5 litres of wine or beer.
South Korea: 2 bottles, no more than 2 litres and US$400 in total.
Philippines: 2 bottles of liquor, with a total value under ₱10,000.
Brazil counts units: 24 units by air or sea, with a maximum of 12 of any one type.
Iceland counts alcohol units: for example 1 litre of spirits, 0.75 litre of wine and 3 litres of beer, or 18 litres of beer on its own.
Sri Lanka: two regular-size bottles of wine and 1.5 litres of spirits for tourists.

Where you can bring the most

UK: 42 litres of beer, 18 litres of still wine and 4 litres of spirits.
Switzerland: 5 litres up to 18% plus 1 litre over 18%, per person per day.
Costa Rica and the Dominican Republic: 5 litres of wine, spirits or liquor per adult.
Oman: 48 cans of beer plus 2 litres of wine — for non-Muslims only.
🔞
Minimum ages differ. 17 in the UK, New Zealand and Switzerland; 18 in most countries; 19 in South Korea; 20 in Iceland (and 20 in Norway for drinks over 22%); 21 in the US and Indonesia. Under the limit age you get no alcohol allowance at all.

What happens if you go over the alcohol limit

Going over rarely makes the bottles illegal. It means duty, seizure, or — in a few countries — worse.

Australia: duty on all the alcohol, not just the excess.
Singapore: anything above the set combination is taxed.
UAE: excess quantities are confiscated.
Maldives: all alcohol is confiscated at the airport, including bottles bought in duty-free.
Thailand: anything above 1 litre is dutiable and must be declared.
China: carrying 1.5 litres or more of drinks at 12% or above puts you in the red channel, and duty is charged above the limit.
Egypt: the litre you buy as a tourist is recorded in your passport.
✈️
Getting the bottles home is a separate rule. In hand luggage, a bottle over 100 ml only passes security if it is sealed in a duty-free security bag with the receipt — see Is a Duty-Free Bag an Extra Carry-On? and the 100ml liquids rule.

Before you fly — checklist

1
Check the alcohol allowance for the country you are landing in, not the one you are leaving.
2
Check the unit: litres, bottles, or alcohol units.
3
Check whether spirits, wine and beer stack or share one choice.
4
Check your age against the minimum age, and any ban on alcohol entirely.
5
Going over? Declare it and pay — undeclared alcohol is seized.
6
For tobacco, see Duty-Free Tobacco Allowance by Country (2026). For the value limit and cash, see Duty-Free Allowance by Country (2026).

Alcohol allowances change, and each customs authority has the final word. Always confirm the current limit with the customs agency of the country you are entering before you travel. Updated 2026.
