Do You Need a Passport to Go to Canada, Mexico, the Bahamas or Puerto Rico? (US Citizens 2026)

Entry Permits·Updated October 2026·7 min read

If you fly to Canada, Mexico or the Bahamas, yes: a U.S. citizen needs a passport book. Puerto Rico is the exception, because it is part of the United States and no passport is needed. By land or sea, Canada and Mexico also accept a passport card and some other documents, and the Bahamas does not accept a passport card at all. Everything below is from the State Department, CBP, TSA and the Canadian and Bahamian immigration pages.

📘 Passport book, passport card or something else?

The rules for coming home come from CBP’s Western Hemisphere Travel Initiative. By air, a U.S. citizen needs a passport book or a trusted traveler card (NEXUS, SENTRI or FAST). By land or sea the list is longer: passport, passport card, enhanced driver’s license, or a trusted traveler card. The passport card is the cheaper wallet-size version, but it never works for a flight. An enhanced driver’s license is issued only by some states and also never works for a flight.

Closed-loop cruises (they begin and end at the same U.S. port) are the one case where a birth certificate and a government photo ID get a U.S. citizen back into the country. CBP adds that you may still need a passport for the countries the ship visits, so check with your cruise line.

Canada: passport book to fly, more choices by road

✈️
Flying to Canada: take a passport book. The State Department says U.S. citizens can enter with a valid passport, passport card or NEXUS card, and adds that a passport card cannot be used on domestic flights within Canada.

Canada’s own immigration site says it is recommended to always travel with a valid passport, the only reliable and universally accepted travel and ID document. If you have no passport and are not travelling by air, you can present identity documents that show your full name, date of birth and citizenship. Whatever Canada lets you in with, you need a document CBP accepts to come back (see the table above), and CBP’s list for adults does not include a birth certificate with a driver’s license. The State Department advises U.S. citizens to review Canada’s requirements for travelling with minors, and there is no tourist visa for stays under 180 days.

Mexico: passport book to fly, plus an FMM permit

The State Department says you need a passport book to fly to Mexico and cannot board a plane with a passport card. Mexican authorities issue a digital entry permit (FMMD) at every international airport, and immigration officers decide how long you can stay. By land you need evidence of citizenship, such as a passport book or card, and you must apply for an FMM entry permit online or at an INM office, unless you stay within 12 miles (20 km) of the U.S. border for 6 days or less. By sea you need a passport, passport card, trusted traveler card or REAL ID, and on a closed-loop cruise a certified birth certificate with a raised seal plus a photo ID if you are 16 or older. A private boat needs a permit from the Mexican government before you arrive.

CBP’s list for getting back into the U.S. does not include a REAL ID, so carry one of the documents in the table above for the return. Mexico’s own migration site only offers the FMM form, so this section rests on the State Department’s Mexico page. For safety warnings see travel warnings for Mexico.

The Bahamas: passport book, valid six months

The Bahamas Immigration Department says all persons entering must present a passport valid for at least six months, and that passport cards are not accepted. You also need a return ticket or other means of leaving, enough money for the stay (or a local sponsor), and a completed Bahamas Immigration Disembarkation/Embarkation Card before arrival. The department adds that admittance is not automatic. The State Department lists no visa for a U.S. citizen staying one month or less, and says a cruise passenger may use a WHTI-compliant document instead of a passport. For a closed-loop cruise that means a birth certificate and photo ID, but the Bahamas Immigration page itself only mentions passports, so a passport book is the document no one will question.

Puerto Rico: no passport, but bring ID

🌴
No passport for Puerto Rico. CBP says U.S. territories are considered part of the United States, so a U.S. citizen travelling directly from one has not left the country and does not need a passport. The WHTI rules do not affect travel between the U.S. mainland and Puerto Rico.

Flights are still screened by TSA. Adults 18 and over must show valid ID at the checkpoint, and since 7 May 2025 a state driver’s license or ID that is not REAL ID compliant is no longer accepted. A passport book or card, a trusted traveler card and a military ID also work. From 1 February 2026, TSA says travellers with no acceptable ID can pay $45 for TSA ConfirmID, which tries to verify identity but is not a guarantee. See flying without a REAL ID.

❓ Quick answers

Do I need a passport to go to Canada?

To fly, yes: a passport book (a NEXUS card also works). By land or sea the State Department lists a passport, passport card or NEXUS card, and Canada accepts other ID showing name, date of birth and citizenship if you have no passport. You still need a document CBP accepts to come back.

Do I need a passport to go to Mexico?

To fly, yes, and it must be a passport book, because a passport card does not work for boarding. By land you need proof of citizenship such as a passport book or card plus an FMM permit, unless you stay within 12 miles (20 km) of the border for 6 days or less.

Do I need a passport to go to the Bahamas?

Yes. The Bahamas Immigration Department requires a passport valid for at least six months and does not accept passport cards. The State Department says a cruise passenger may use a WHTI-compliant document instead.

Do I need a passport to go to Puerto Rico?

No. Puerto Rico is a U.S. territory, so a U.S. citizen does not need a passport. You still need a REAL ID or other TSA-accepted ID to fly.

Do I need a passport for a cruise?

On a closed-loop cruise, CBP says U.S. citizens can return with a birth certificate and a photo ID, but you may still need a passport for the countries the ship visits. Check with your cruise line.

Can I drive to Canada or Mexico without a passport?

Yes, with a passport card, an enhanced driver’s license or a trusted traveler card, which CBP accepts for land and sea. Mexico also requires an FMM permit beyond the border zone.

Do children need a passport?

Children also need their own passport to fly. By land or sea, U.S. children under 16 (under 19 with a school, religious or other youth group) need only a birth certificate or other proof of citizenship.
