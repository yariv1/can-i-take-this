Flying with a Vape: The Country-by-Country Minefield

Vapes & E-Cigs · Updated 2026 · 9 min read

A vape that is perfectly legal in London can get you arrested in Bangkok. The device in your pocket does not change — the rules around it change completely depending on where you land. Unlike the 100ml liquid rule, there is no global standard. Every country sets its own law, and the penalties range from confiscation to criminal prosecution.

This is what you need to check before every trip.

✈️ The one rule that applies everywhere

⚠️
Vapes and e-cigarettes must travel in the cabin — never in checked luggage. The lithium battery is a fire risk in an unmonitored hold. This applies on every airline, on every route, globally. Violation can result in the bag being offloaded or a fine.
✅Carry the device in your hand luggage or on your person
✅E-liquid in containers under 100ml, in your liquids bag
❌No vapes in checked bags — lithium battery fire risk
❌No vaping on board — banned on all commercial flights worldwide

EU countries follow TPD regulations: nicotine max 20mg/ml, tank capacity max 2ml, 10ml refill bottles. USA requires 21+ and FDA-authorised products. UK is the most permissive — actively promotes vaping as a smoking cessation tool.

⚠️ Complex — legal but conditional

🇦🇺 Australia
🇯🇵 Japan
🇦🇪 UAE
🇹🇷 Turkey
🇮🇩 Indonesia
🇦🇺
Australia: Nicotine vapes require a prescription from an Australian doctor. Recreational nicotine e-liquid is illegal without one. Nicotine-free devices are legal. Importing nicotine e-liquid for personal use without a prescription can result in seizure at the border.
🇯🇵
Japan: Nicotine e-liquid is classified as a medicine and is effectively banned for consumers. Nicotine-free vaping is legal. Heated tobacco products (iQOS, glo, IQOS) are widely available and legal — many visitors use those instead.
🇦🇪
UAE: Legalised in 2019 after a prior ban. Vapes are sold in licensed shops. Public vaping is restricted — same rules as smoking. Do not vape near mosques, government buildings, or in enclosed public spaces.

❗
Thailand is the highest-risk destination. Possession of a vape is illegal and can result in up to 10 years in prison or a fine of up to 500,000 baht. Police actively enforce this. Tourists are not exempt. Singapore carries fines of up to SGD 2,000 for possession. India banned all e-cigarettes in 2019 — import, sale, and possession are all offences.

🛂 What happens if customs finds it

⚠️
Confiscation — the minimum outcome in most ban countries. The device and any e-liquid is seized. No compensation.
💸
On-the-spot fine — common in Singapore, Qatar, Kuwait. Amounts vary; can reach several thousand dollars.
🚔
Arrest and prosecution — possible in Thailand, India, Brazil, Philippines. Criminal record implications even for tourists.
✈️
Deportation — rare but documented, particularly in Southeast Asia for repeat or large-quantity cases.
💡
Transit counts. If your connecting flight stops in Bangkok or Singapore — even airside — having a vape in your carry-on puts you in scope of local law. Some enforcement happens at transit security. Do not assume airside transit is safe in ban countries.

📋 Before you fly — checklist

1Check the destination country status — legal, complex, or banned
2Check every transit country too — not just the final destination
3E-liquid in 100ml or smaller containers, in your liquids bag
4Device in cabin bag — never in the hold
5For Australia: carry your prescription or leave nicotine liquid at home
6For Japan: switch to a nicotine-free device or heated tobacco for the trip
7For any ban country: leave the device at home — no exceptions

Rules change. Countries that permitted vaping have reversed course, and vice versa. Check official government travel advisories for your destination before every trip — not just once.
