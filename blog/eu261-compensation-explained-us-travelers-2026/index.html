EU261 Compensation 2026: Does It Apply to Your Flight, How Much, and How to Claim

Airline Fees·Updated October 2026·9 min read

EU261 pays cash when a flight leaves Europe late, is cancelled or you are bumped, and it covers American travelers on US airlines flying from Europe to the U.S. It does not cover the flight over on those airlines. The law is Regulation (EC) No 261/2004 of the European Union. It pays €250, €400 or €600 by distance for a delay of 3 hours or more at arrival, for a cancellation you were told about less than 14 days before, and for denied boarding. It covers any flight that leaves an EU airport, plus flights that arrive in the EU on an EU airline. A Delta flight from New York to Paris is not covered. The same Delta flight from Paris to New York is.

🧭 Does EU261 cover your flight?

It follows the flight, not your passport and not where you bought the ticket. “EU” means the European Union’s member countries, not all of Europe. The rules also apply to flights to and from Iceland, Norway and Switzerland. The UK has its own copy of the rules after Brexit.

The airline that operates the flight is the one responsible, which can differ from the airline on your ticket. If a flight is sold by one airline and flown by another, you claim against the one that flew you. You must also have a confirmed booking and check in at the time the airline told you in writing, or at least 45 minutes before departure if it gave no time. And if you already received compensation or assistance under the law of a country outside the EU for the same trip, EU261 does not add a second payment.

💶 How much EU261 pays

The amount depends on distance, not on your ticket price. For delays, Your Europe lists a 3-hour line at arrival, and for the longest flights a split at 4 hours. For cancellations and denied boarding the amounts are €250, €400 and €600 by distance.

Delay money is paid on how late you reach your final destination. A cancellation counts when you were told less than 14 days before departure; the airline must also offer a refund or a new flight. Denied boarding for overbooking pays the same amounts. UK law sets £220, £350 or £520 for the same distances. The airline also owes meals and, if you wait overnight, a hotel. For a delay at departure the meal and phone-call right starts after 2, 3 or 4 hours depending on distance, and a delay of 5 hours or more also gives you the right to a refund and a return flight. 🌦️ When the airline owes you nothing

The airline can refuse when the delay or cancellation was caused by extraordinary circumstances. It has to prove two things: that the event caused the problem, and that it could not have been avoided even if it had taken every reasonable measure. A weather excuse is not enough on its own.

The airline can be excused whenThe airline is NOT excused by
  • Air traffic management decisions.
  • Adverse weather.
  • Political instability.
  • Security risks.
  • A strike outside the airline, such as at an airport.
  • Most technical problems found during maintenance, or caused by poor maintenance.
  • A collision of mobile boarding stairs with the aircraft.
  • A strike by the airline’s own staff.
  • An unexpected breakdown that comes with the normal running of an aircraft.

The European Commission’s guidelines say a technical problem is not an extraordinary circumstance even if it was unexpected and not caused by poor maintenance, when it comes with the normal running of the aircraft. Denied boarding for safety, security or health reasons, or for missing travel documents, also does not pay.

📨 How to claim

1
Ask for the written notice at the airport. The law says an airline must display a notice at check-in, and must give every passenger who was denied boarding, had a cancellation, or had a delay of 2 hours or more a written notice of the rules and the contact details of the national authority.
2
Complain first to the airline that operated the flight, using its complaint form. If you bought through a travel agent, the airline that flew you is still the one responsible.
3
No reply in 2 months, or not satisfied? Complain to the national authority of the country where the incident happened. It gives a non-binding legal opinion.
4
Alternative dispute resolution is open only to EU residents. Otherwise you can use the European Small Claims procedure in court; for an EU airline you can file where the flight arrived or departed.

Is there a deadline? The regulation sets no time limit. The Commission’s guidelines say it is national law in each EU country, and the 2-year limit of the Montreal Convention does not apply to these claims. That means the limit depends on the country, and no official table lists them, so claim early and check the country’s rule.

🗳️ The 2026 reform: what changes and when

Parliament and Council agreed a new law in June 2026 and the Council gave final clearance on 13 July. For travelers it keeps the 3-hour delay line and the 250, 400 and 600 euro amounts, and adds clearer rights to assistance: refreshments every 2 hours, a meal after 3 hours and every 5 hours after that (up to three a day), internet access, two phone calls and a hotel if you must stay overnight. Airlines must tell you the cause of the disruption, and offer a new route within 3 hours when you choose to be re-routed after a cancellation or denied boarding. The law also clarifies what counts as an extraordinary circumstance, bans refusing the return flight because you skipped the outbound one, and makes airlines show fares with an allowance for a piece of hand baggage.

Before you fly home from Europe

Keep the booking confirmation and boarding pass: they show the airline, flight and times.
Write down the real arrival time at your final destination. A delay claim is based on it.
Arrive at check-in at the time your airline gave you; the 45-minute rule only applies if it gave none.
Keep receipts for meals, hotel and rides if the airline did not provide them.
Ask the airline for the cause of the delay in writing: weather and a technical fault lead to different answers.

❓ Quick answers

What is EU261?

EU261 is the common name for Regulation (EC) No 261/2004, the European Union law that gives passengers fixed cash compensation and care when a flight is delayed at arrival, cancelled or overbooked. It covers flights leaving an EU airport on any airline and flights arriving in the EU on an EU airline.

Does EU261 apply to US airlines?

Yes, on flights that leave an EU airport: a Delta, American or United flight from Paris, Madrid or Rome to the U.S. is covered. No, on flights from the U.S. to the EU on a US airline, which are not covered.

Does EU261 apply to flights from the US to Europe?

Only when an EU airline operates the flight, such as Lufthansa or Air France. A US airline flight from the U.S. into the EU is not covered.

How much is EU261 compensation?

It is 250 euros for flights of 1,500 km or less, 400 euros for flights of 1,500 to 3,500 km and longer flights inside the EU, and 600 euros for longer flights. For a delay on a flight over 3,500 km the amount is 300 euros if you arrive 3 to 4 hours late and 600 euros from 4 hours.

How long does a delay have to be for EU261?

You reach your final destination 3 hours or more late. Meals and phone calls start earlier, after 2, 3 or 4 hours at departure depending on distance.

Is bad weather an extraordinary circumstance?

Adverse weather is one of the events the EU lists as extraordinary circumstances, but the airline must prove that the weather caused the delay or cancellation and that it could not have been avoided with every reasonable measure. Most technical faults and the airline’s own staff strikes do not count.

How do I claim EU261?

Send your complaint to the airline that operated the flight with its form. If there is no reply within 2 months, or you are not satisfied, complain to the national authority of the country where it happened.

Is there a deadline to claim EU261?

The regulation sets none. The time limit comes from the national law of the EU country, so it varies, and no single official table exists.

Does EU261 apply to the UK?

No. The UK has its own version, UK261, for flights leaving a UK airport on any airline and for flights arriving in the UK on an EU or UK airline. It pays 220, 350 or 520 pounds.

Is EU261 changing in 2026?

Parliament and Council agreed a new law and the Council gave final clearance on 13 July 2026. It keeps the 3-hour line and the amounts and adds clearer rights. It takes effect 12 months and 20 days after publication in the Official Journal, so claims today run under the current rules.

EU and UK rules change; the airline’s own pages and the official pages below on the day you travel are the ones that count.
