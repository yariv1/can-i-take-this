Flying with CBD & Medical Cannabis (2026)

Medications · Updated 2026 · 8 min read

"CBD" and "medical cannabis" get treated as one topic and they are not. One is a near-zero-THC hemp extract that is legal to carry in most of the US and much of Europe. The other is a THC-containing product that stays federally illegal to fly with in the US no matter which state issued your card, and is a criminal matter in a long list of countries regardless of what your doctor prescribed at home.

Hemp CBD vs. medical cannabis — not the same rulebook

🌿
Hemp-derived CBD, under 0.3% THC — permitted in carry-on and checked bags on US domestic flights, subject to the standard 100ml liquids rule in carry-on. A printed Certificate of Analysis (COA) isn't required but headed off more questions than it causes.
🚫
Medical cannabis (THC-containing) — still federally illegal to fly with in the US, regardless of a state medical card. TSA updated its guidance in April 2026 to note some FDA-approved cannabinoid products may get special handling, but has not published quantity limits or documentation rules — treat this as unresolved, not permitted.
⚠️
If it's found at security, it becomes a local matter. Airport checkpoints answer to the jurisdiction they sit in, not to your home state. What happens next depends entirely on that airport's local law, not on your prescription.

A medical card does not cross a border

This is the part that catches travellers who've never had a problem flying between legal-cannabis states. A US medical marijuana card carries zero legal weight the moment you land somewhere else — international law follows the destination, not your diagnosis.

Japan — illegal for any use, medical or recreational; customs screening is intensive and citizens can face charges for use committed abroad South Korea — illegal outside a narrow prescribed-access scheme; foreign medical cards are not recognised Indonesia — treated as a serious narcotic; trafficking quantities can carry the death penalty Malaysia — same category as hard narcotics; long imprisonment for possession Russia — banned for any use; even hemp CBD oil has led to arrests at customs Bahamas — illegal; a US medical card offers no protection from prosecution, even for small quantities

Where even CBD — not just THC — is the problem

The Middle East doesn't distinguish between hemp CBD and recreational cannabis the way the US and EU do. That difference matters more there than almost anywhere else.

UAE — CBD is illegal; no legal distinction from THC-containing cannabis products Saudi Arabia — CBD is illegal, treated with the same severity as other narcotics

Europe: legal, but not one rule

CBD is broadly legal across Europe, but the THC ceiling that makes it legal shifts by country — and an ingestible product faces separate food-safety rules on top of that.

Before you fly — checklist

1
Know which product you're carrying — hemp CBD under 0.3% THC and medical cannabis are legally different, even if both came from a dispensary.
2
Never fly domestically in the US with any THC-containing product, medical card or not — it remains federally illegal.
3
Carry a printed Certificate of Analysis for hemp CBD — it's not required, but it answers the question before it's asked.
4
For international trips, check the destination's rules on CBD specifically, not just cannabis — some countries ban CBD outright.
5
A US medical marijuana card has no legal standing outside the US — do not rely on it anywhere else.
6
When in doubt, buy CBD at your destination instead of carrying it across a border.

Cannabis and CBD law changes quickly and varies by product, THC percentage, and country, with penalties that can be severe. Always confirm current requirements with the destination's embassy or health ministry before you travel. Updated 2026.
