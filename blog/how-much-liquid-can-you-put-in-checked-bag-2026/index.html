How Much Liquid Can You Put in a Checked Bag? Limits, Banned Items & Packing Tips (2026)

Packing Rules · Updated 2026 · 9 min read

In a checked bag there is no 3.4 oz (100 ml) limit: full-size shampoo, lotion, sunscreen, drinks and sauces can all go in. The limits are different. Toiletries and aerosols are capped at 500 ml (17 fl oz) per container and 2 L (70 oz) in total per person, alcohol over 24% is capped at 5 L, and some liquids are not allowed in a checked bag at all: fuel, cooking spray, spray paint, bleach and more.

⚖️ The caps that do apply in a checked bag

1
Toiletries and aerosols: 500 ml per container, 2 L in total. The FAA rule covers “medicinal and toilet articles for personal use (including aerosols)”: no more than 2 kg or 2 L (70 oz) per person in total, and no single container above 0.5 kg or 500 ml (17 fl oz). TSA repeats it on its pages for perfume, sunscreen, deodorant, hair spray and hand sanitizer. It matters most when you pack several full-size bottles.
2
Alcohol: by strength. 24% alcohol or less (most beer and wine): no limit. More than 24% and up to 70%: 5 L (1.3 gallons) per person, in unopened retail packaging. More than 70% (140 proof): not allowed. Check with your airline before you pack any alcohol. Allowances at the arrival airport are separate: see duty-free alcohol allowance by country.
3
Dry ice: 5.5 lb. Vented, marked packaging, and the airline has to approve it.
4
Pepper spray: one 4 fl oz can. With a safety mechanism, in a checked bag only.
⚠️
Pressurised cans: toiletry aerosols are fine up to the caps above. Cooking spray, spray paint, spray starch and bear spray are the ones that are not allowed at all, and aerosol insecticide is allowed in a checked bag only if it is not labeled hazardous material. See aerosols on a plane.

🚫 Liquids you cannot pack in any bag

These are on TSA’s list as not allowed in carry-on and not allowed in checked bags:

⛽
Fuels: gasoline, lighter fluid, cooking fuel, butane, propane.
🎨
Paint products: spray paint, flammable paint, turpentine and paint thinner.
🧴
Household chemicals: liquid bleach, fertilizer, spray starch, cooking spray.
🥃
Alcohol over 70% (140 proof), bear spray and tear gas, fireworks, fire extinguishers.

If a product carries a flammable, corrosive or toxic warning symbol, assume it is not allowed and check the FAA PackSafe page before you pack it. The final decision always rests with the airline and TSA.

🧳 How to pack liquids so they do not leak

Checked bags go through belts, carts and cargo holds, and TSA may open and inspect them. These are packing tips, not rules:

1
Put every bottle in its own sealed bag, or one bag for each group. A split bottle then ruins one bag, not the suitcase.
2
Keep bottles upright and padded in the middle of the suitcase, wrapped in clothes, and not against the shell.
3
Wrap glass (perfume, oil, sauces, wine) and put it in a shoe or a padded pouch.
4
Screw caps tight. Add tape over flip-tops and pumps, or turn the pump head to the locked position.
5
Do not fill bottles to the brim: pressure changes in the hold can push liquid out of the cap.
6
Weigh the bag. Liquids are heavy, and a litre weighs about 1 kg. Airline weight limits depend on the airline and fare.

❓ Quick answers

Is there a limit on liquids in checked baggage?

There is no 3.4 oz limit. The limits are the 500 ml per container and 2 L total for toiletries and aerosols, 5 L for alcohol between 24% and 70%, and the list of items that are not allowed.

Can I put a full-size shampoo in a checked bag?

Yes. TSA lists shampoo, conditioner, toothpaste, lotion and cream as allowed in checked bags.

Can I pack perfume in a checked bag?

Yes, up to 500 ml (17 fl oz) per bottle and 2 L (70 oz) in total with your other toiletries. Wrap the glass.

Is the 3-1-1 rule for checked bags?

No. The 3-1-1 rule is for carry-on bags only. See how many ounces you can bring on a plane.

What if my liquid is over 100 ml and I only have a carry-on?

Medicine, baby milk and food and duty-free liquids can go over 3.4 oz if you declare them. For other products, check the 100 ml rule and your options.

Before you fly — checklist

1
Move bottles over 3.4 oz from your carry-on to the checked bag.
2
Keep each toiletry or aerosol container at 500 ml (17 fl oz) or less, and all of them together at 2 L (70 oz) or less.
3
Check the strength of any alcohol: over 24% is capped at 5 L, over 70% is banned.
4
Leave out fuels, spray paint, cooking spray, bleach and anything with a flammable warning.
5
Seal, pad and bag every bottle, and weigh the suitcase.

The final decision rests with the airline and TSA. Rules outside the US differ; confirm current requirements with your airline and TSA before you travel. Updated 2026.
