⚖️ Do not travel countries: what Level 4 means and whether you can still go

🇺🇸
United States, Level 4: Do not travel. The highest of four levels. Many Level 4 advisories also say the US government has very limited ability, or none, to help Americans there. The card for each country shows what that advisory says.
🇨🇦
Canada, Avoid all travel. Canada’s wording: you should not travel to this country, territory or region because your personal safety and security are at great risk, and if you are already there you should think about leaving if it is safe to do so.
🇬🇧
UK, FCDO advises against all travel. The UK has its own scale: “advises against all travel” is its top warning, “advises against all but essential travel” is one step lower, and either can apply to the whole country or only to parts of it.
📜
These are advice, not a legal ban. No government stops you at the border for traveling to a Level 4 country. Some advisories do add real rules on top of the advice, such as a passport restriction or a flight rule for travelers returning to the US. Those appear in the cards as “Rule beyond the advice”.
🛡️
Insurance is the practical limit. The UK government warns that your travel insurance could be invalidated if you travel against FCDO advice. Check your own policy before you book: only your insurer can tell you what is covered.

🧳 If you decide to travel anyway, or you are already there

🚪
Plan an exit that does not depend on an embassy. For Libya the State Department says it cannot offer emergency services, and it advises travelers to high-risk areas to have their own plan to leave. Read the “help if something goes wrong” line on the card of your destination.
📝
Prepare the paperwork. For high-risk destinations the State Department advises a will and end-of-life instructions, a power of attorney, and sharing important documents and logins with someone at home.
📞
Agree a communication plan. The State Department also advises a “proof of life” protocol with family for kidnapping or detention, and leaving a DNA sample with a medical provider.
📍
Enroll with your government. US citizens can enroll a trip in STEP, the State Department’s Smart Traveler Enrollment Program; Canadians can register a trip with Global Affairs Canada. Neither is a guarantee of rescue.
🩺
Check insurance and medical evacuation. Read the exclusions for war, terrorism and travel against advice, and confirm medical evacuation is covered. Many Level 4 advisories describe very limited local health care.
🔁
Check again the day you leave. A level can change within hours in a crisis. This page re-checks every 6 hours, but your government’s own page is the final word.

❓ Quick answers

What is a Level 4 travel advisory?

It is the highest of the four levels used by the US State Department, called “Do Not Travel”. It is used where the risks can be life-threatening or where the US government has very limited ability to help Americans. It is advice, not a legal ban.

Which countries have a Level 4 travel advisory?

The section “Countries with a Level 4 travel advisory right now” above lists every destination the State Department rates Level 4, with its reason. The list is rebuilt from the official feed every 6 hours, so it is current.

What are the “do not travel” countries?

“Do not travel” is the State Department’s wording for Level 4. The UK says “advises against all travel” and Canada says “avoid all travel”. The cards show all three for each country.

What is the difference between Level 3 and Level 4?

Level 3 is “reconsider travel” and Level 4 is “do not travel”. Level 4 is used for the most serious risks, and often where the US can provide little or no emergency help.

Is it illegal to travel to a Level 4 country?

Generally no: advisories are advice. Some advisories add real rules, for example a restriction on using a US passport for a destination or a rule for flying back to the US. Those are shown on the country’s card as “Rule beyond the advice”.

Will my travel insurance cover me in a Level 4 country?

Often not by default. The UK government warns that insurance could be invalidated if you travel against FCDO advice. Read your policy’s exclusions and ask your insurer before you book.

What should I do if I am already in a Level 4 country?

Read the card for your country: some advisories tell citizens to leave immediately, and Canada says to think about leaving if it is safe to do so. Enroll with your government’s traveler program, tell family where you are, and follow instructions from your embassy.

Where does the reason for each country come from?

From the US State Department advisory itself: the “due to” line in the headline and the statements about embassy help. The UK status comes from the GOV.UK foreign travel advice API and Canada’s from its travel advice data. Nothing is typed in by hand.
