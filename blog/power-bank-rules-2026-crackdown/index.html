Power Banks on Planes: What Actually Changed in 2026

Batteries & Electronics · Updated 2026 · 7 min read

If you have flown this year, you may have noticed a flight attendant asking passengers to keep power banks out of the overhead bins. You are not imagining it. 2026 is the year the rules changed more than they had in the previous decade — and they changed almost everywhere at once.

Your power bank still belongs in your carry-on, never in checked baggage. That part has not moved. What is new is how many you can bring, where it can sit, and whether you can use it at all once the doors close.

The one rule that now applies almost everywhere

The International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO) — the UN body whose standards filter into law across 193 countries — set the first coordinated international standard on portable batteries in the cabin, effective 27 March 2026.

🌐
ICAO Standard — 27 March 2026
  • Max two power banks per passenger — a hard cap on international flights, not a guideline.
  • No in-flight charging — no using it to charge your phone or laptop once airborne.
  • No overhead bins — must stay in a seat pocket, under the seat, or on your person.

The logic: a power bank that starts smoking in your lap is dealt with in seconds. One sealed in a closed overhead bin is discovered only when smoke pours out — by which point the fire is self-sustaining. This is the floor. Airlines and countries have stacked stricter rules on top.

The numbers that still decide everything

Underneath all the new rules, capacity limits are unchanged. Watt-hours (Wh) are what security measures — not mAh, not "size".

Green zone
< 100 Wh
≈ up to 27,000 mAh
✓ Carry-on OK
Amber zone
100–160 Wh
27,000–43,000 mAh
⚠ Airline approval
Red zone
> 160 Wh
43,000+ mAh
✗ Banned entirely

Most chargers show mAh, not Wh. The formula: Wh = (mAh × 3.7) ÷ 1000. A 20,000mAh bank works out to 74Wh — comfortably in the green zone. Our power bank guide walks through the full maths.

Why now? The incidents that forced the crackdown

Unlike a phone or laptop, a bare power bank has no thermal management and no fault software. If a cell is damaged or badly made, it can enter thermal runaway — and once it does, there is no built-in system to stop it.

How to pack so you sail through

1
Carry-on only, always. Never in checked baggage — global, absolute rule.
2
Bring no more than two (one if flying Southwest). Each ideally under 100Wh.
3
Keep them reachable. Seat pocket, under-seat bag, or on you. Assume overhead bins are off-limits.
4
Don't plan to charge in flight. On Lufthansa Group and Japan routes you simply cannot — charge before boarding.
5
Protect the terminals. Original packaging, a pouch, or tape over contacts — prevent shorts against your keys.
6
Check both ends of your trip. Rules vary by carrier and country — what was fine outbound may not be on the return.
