Power Bank Rules by Airline (2026): 33 Airlines Compared

Batteries & Electronics · Updated 2026 · 8 min read

Every airline has its own power bank rule now, and they no longer match. One lets you charge your phone from the bank. The next bans using it at all. A few allow only one bank. The only constant is that a power bank goes in your carry-on, never in the checked bag.

Below are 33 airlines in one searchable table. Each row has what the airline allows in flight, how many banks and how big, where the bank has to sit, and a link to the airline’s own page. If the airline is not on the list, our power bank guide covers the general rules, and United and Southwest are covered there.

🚫 Which airlines ban power bank use in flight?

23 of the 33 airlines in the table ban using or charging a power bank in flight. The bans cluster by region. Emirates, Etihad, Singapore Airlines, Cathay Pacific, Thai, EVA Air, Korean Air and Japan Airlines ban it in Asia and the Middle East. So do Lufthansa Group, KLM, Air France, SAS and Aer Lingus in Europe, and Qantas and Virgin Australia in Oceania.

The ban usually means two things at once: you cannot charge the bank from the seat power or USB port, and you cannot use it to charge your phone or laptop. If you need power in the air, charge the device from the in-seat outlet or USB port where the seat has one.

✅ Which airlines still allow you to use a power bank?

A few airlines in the table do not ban use outright. They set limits on when and how.

1
Ryanair and Delta: you can charge a device from the bank, but not during taxi, take-off or landing. Neither lets you recharge the bank itself on board.
2
Qatar Airways and Air New Zealand: use is limited to some phases of flight and the bank cannot be recharged on board.
3
British Airways, American Airlines and Pegasus: we found no in-flight ban in the rule we read. BA and American say not to charge the bank from seat power.
4
ANA: it asks passengers to refrain from charging other devices from the bank, and bans charging the bank from the seat outlet.

Do not treat this list as permanent. Airlines in this group have been the ones moving toward a ban, so check the airline’s page again close to your flight date.

☝️ Which airlines allow only one power bank?

Most airlines allow two power banks per passenger. Emirates, Etihad and Wizz Air allow only one, each up to 100 Wh. If you travel with a phone bank and a laptop bank, one of them stays at home or goes in the airline’s other approved storage.

🔌 Which airlines accept power banks up to 160 Wh?

The common cap is 100 Wh, about a 27,000 mAh bank. Some airlines let bigger banks on board with limits:

Up to 160 Wh, max 2: easyJet, Korean Air, ANA, Japan Airlines, Qantas, Virgin Australia 100 to 160 Wh with airline approval: Lufthansa Group, Air France, Singapore Airlines, Scoot, EVA Air, AirAsia 100 Wh or less only: KLM, British Airways, Ryanair, SAS, Aer Lingus, Wizz Air, Pegasus, Icelandair, Turkish, Emirates, Etihad, Qatar, Thai, Cathay, Air New Zealand, LATAM, JetBlue, Delta, American Different rule: Air Canada allows max 2 banks in the 100 to 160 Wh range, and Air China requires the 3C mark on domestic China flights

To check your own bank, the formula is Wh = mAh × 3.7 ÷ 1000. A 20,000 mAh bank is 74 Wh. Japan Airlines has warned its 160 Wh limit may drop to 100 Wh from January 2027, so a big bank that flies today may not next year.

Before you fly — checklist

1
Power banks go in the carry-on bag, never in the checked bag.
2
Check the Wh label. No visible rating can mean the bank is refused.
3
Count your banks. Two is the usual limit. Emirates, Etihad and Wizz Air allow one.
4
Keep it in the seat pocket or under the seat in front, never in the overhead bin.
5
Assume you cannot use it in flight. Charge your devices before boarding and use the seat power instead.
6
Connecting flights or partner airlines? The strictest airline on your ticket sets the rule for that leg.
7
Bringing a vape or e-cigarette too? See vape rules by country, since its battery follows similar rules.
