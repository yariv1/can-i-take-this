How Many Ounces Can You Bring on a Plane? 3.4 oz Carry-On Limit, Size Chart & Checked Bags (2026)

Packing Rules · Updated 2026 · 8 min read

In a carry-on, the limit is 3.4 ounces (100 ml) per container, and every container has to fit in one quart-size bag. That is the answer to “how many ounces can you bring on a plane”. In a checked bag there is no 3.4 oz rule, but toiletries and aerosols are capped at 500 ml (17 fl oz) per container and 2 L (70 oz) in total.

📏 Check your size

Use the size printed on the container. If the label shows both ounces and millilitres, use the millilitres.

The checked-bag column is for toiletries and aerosols, the products the 500 ml cap applies to. Alcohol, fuel and pressurised cans have their own rules; see aerosols on a plane.

🧴 What counts as one 3.4 oz item

The limit is written per container: 3.4 ounces (100 milliliters) or less per item. Two things follow from that.

1
The printed size counts, not what is left. A half-empty 6 oz bottle is still a 6 oz bottle.
2
There is no total-ounces limit in the carry-on. You can bring as many 3.4 oz containers as fit in one quart-size bag. How many that is depends on their shape; TSA does not publish a number.
3
Tubes, jars and sprays all count. If you can pour, spray, squeeze, spread or smear it, it is a liquid. Search 93 products in Is It a Liquid? The full TSA list.

🧳 Checked bags: the ounce limits

Full-size bottles are fine in a checked bag. The one cap TSA quotes on its item pages for perfume, nail polish, deodorant, sunscreen and hand sanitizer is the FAA’s: 2 kg or 2 L (70 oz) in total per person, and no single container above 0.5 kg or 500 ml (about 17 fl oz).

⚠️
Drinks and food are not covered by that cap, but alcohol between 24% and 70% is limited to 5 L in unopened retail packaging, and airlines have their own weight limits. See the duty-free alcohol allowance by country.

✅ Items that can go over 3.4 oz in a carry-on

Three groups are allowed above 3.4 oz. You must declare them at the start of screening, and they may get extra checks.

💊
Medically necessary liquids, in reasonable quantities for your trip. See the liquid medication exemption.
🍼
Baby formula, breast milk and baby food. See baby food and formula on a plane.
🛍️
Duty-free liquids bought abroad on a connecting flight to the US, in a sealed tamper-evident bag with the receipt. See is a duty-free bag an extra carry-on.

🌍 Millilitres, and flights outside the US

The same limit is written 100 ml in most other countries. This page is TSA’s rule for US airports; details differ by country, and some airports with newer scanners have changed them. For the metric version, see how many ml you can take on a plane.

Before you fly — checklist

1
Read the size on every bottle. 3.4 oz or 100 ml is the most one container can hold.
2
3.5 oz and 4 oz bottles are over the limit, even if they are nearly empty.
3
Everything under the limit goes together in one quart-size bag.
4
Bigger bottles go in the checked bag, up to 500 ml (17 fl oz) per toiletry or aerosol container and 2 L (70 oz) in total.
5
Medicine, baby milk and baby food can go over 3.4 oz. Declare them first.
6
Empty drink bottles before security and refill after.

The final decision rests with the TSA officer at the checkpoint, and rules at airports outside the US differ. Confirm current requirements with TSA before you travel. Updated 2026.
