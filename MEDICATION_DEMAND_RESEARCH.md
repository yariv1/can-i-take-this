# Which medicines must the database have? Demand research (2026-10-20)

User order: research independently of Search Console. We cannot rank for medicines we do not have, so GSC only tells us what we already cover.
Current database: `MED_DB` in `canitakethis.html` = 46 entries / 40 ingredients, chosen by judgement, never checked against demand. Only 14 have a country page.

## Method (all independent of our own site)
1. **Google autocomplete discovery** (`suggestqueries` endpoint, US English): the top-10 predicted completions of "can i bring ...", "can i take ...", "is ... legal in", "can i travel with ...", "can i bring ... to japan", "can i bring ... to dubai" for every 1- and 2-letter prefix (6 x 702 calls). Google lists what people type most, so this finds medicines nobody on our side guessed. Raw ranking: 11,522 distinct completions; the medicine-related top 119 are in `research_med_discovery.tsv` (item, weighted score, templates that produced it: A bring, B take, C legal in, D travel with, E to japan, F to dubai).
2. **Candidate probe** (340 names: the ClinCalc top-200 most prescribed US drugs 2023, our own list, travel kit OTC, controlled drugs customs flag). It SATURATED: Google completes the phrase for any name, so presence says nothing; ignore it as a ranking. Only the discovery run (1) is used for ranking.
3. **Wikipedia pageviews** (12-month average, generic names): popularity of the active ingredient (gabapentin 88K/month, paracetamol 86K, trazodone 61K, sertraline 67K, diazepam 66K, ibuprofen 55K, tirzepatide 46K, ...).
4. **Customs-flag lists** (Global Rescue, Daily Passport, Kiplinger and similar travel press, US embassy text for Japan): pseudoephedrine, codeine, ADHD stimulants, sleep/anxiety drugs, diphenhydramine, Vicks inhaler, CBD/cannabis, tramadol/pregabalin (UAE, Indonesia, Thailand, Singapore). These are press and blog sources: used to choose WHAT to cover, never as the rule itself (rules must come from official pages, per country).
5. **GSC 90 days** (our own queries): only 13 medicine queries with 5+ impressions, all "drug + country" ("adderall lebanon", "sudafed spain", "is adderall legal in uae", "tramadol thailand", "benadryl in colombia", "codeine egypt", "ritalin ireland"). Confirms the dominant intent is "[medicine] + [country]" and "is [medicine] legal in [country]".

## Caveat that decides the build order
A new entry is only useful if the answer is true. For each added medicine the answer needs (a) the airline rule by form (already generic: pills / liquid / needle) and (b) the per-country rule from official pages (the 30+ countries we did before). Tier 1 OTC items inherit the existing generic OTC rule (`MED_RULES` default) and can ship quickly; every country-specific claim stays "no verified info yet, see official source" until read from an official page, as today.
