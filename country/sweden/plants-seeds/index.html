Do not bring plants or seeds without a permit.

  • Live plants, seeds, soil and cuttings are banned or need a permit in almost every country.
  • Where they are allowed, you usually need a phytosanitary certificate from your own country, issued before you travel.
  • Declare them on arrival, and never pack soil.

A phytosanitary certificate is needed for plants, plant products and seeds entering Sweden from outside the EU.

🌱 Plants and seeds: what the EU says for Sweden

Sweden applies the EU plant-health rules.

ItemOfficial EU rule
Plants for planting, cut flowers, other plants and plant productsProhibited in personal luggage unless a phytosanitary certificate accompanies them
Seeds for non-commercial useOnly with a phytosanitary certificate from the country of origin or an EU permit
Fruit and vegetablesCertificate needed, except pineapple, coconut, durian, banana and date; Your Europe mentions a limited quantity but gives no amount
  • The certificate comes from the plant protection authority of the country you bring the plants from, and it also applies to bulbs, cuttings and seeds sent by post.

❓ Quick answers

Can I bring seeds into Sweden?

Only with a phytosanitary certificate, says the European Commission.

Can I bring plants into Sweden from outside the EU?

Not without the certificate, says the Commission, whether they are for planting or are fruit, vegetables or cut flowers.

What Swedish Customs says about protected and prohibited plants

Crossing Sweden’s border with endangered plants, or products made from them, is prohibited without special authorisation. EU and Swedish rules apply on top of the CITES convention, and breaking them can lead to a fine or prison.

Customs adds that some plants need authorisation and certificates, that moving some is completely prohibited, and that soil and other organic growing media are restricted as well.

What the Swedish Board of Agriculture says about plants for private persons

The Swedish Board of Agriculture (Jordbruksverket) says that within the EU, personal purchases of plants or plant products generally need no health certificate, though quarantine pests must never be spread. From outside the EU, most plants for planting and many plant products need a phytosanitary certificate from the exporting country's plant health authority, usually paid for by the traveller, with exemptions for Switzerland, Liechtenstein and Northern Ireland. Fresh pineapple, banana, coconut, date and durian are exempt, as are dried seeds not meant for sowing, dried and frozen produce and processed foods.

Some items cannot be brought in at all from outside the EU, including many conifer seedlings, citrus plants and seed potatoes. The Board inspects regulated plants and seeds at a Swedish border checkpoint or the first EU country they reach, for a fee, and you request the inspection form by email to importkontroll@jordbruksverket.se. Seeds for sowing are inspected if they are on the EU list. Endangered plants fall under CITES permits. The page does not cover soil or penalties.

The Swedish Environmental Protection Agency (Naturvårdsverket) adds a separate rule for invasive species: plants and animals on the EU list of invasive alien species may not be imported, sold, grown, transported or kept alive in Sweden, and its advice to travellers is plain: do not bring seeds or seedlings into Sweden. It also asks people returning from sensitive natural areas, such as the Arctic, to check that luggage, clothing and equipment carry no seeds, plant debris or insects.
