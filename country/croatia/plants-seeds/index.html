🛂 ENTERINGCroatia

High-risk - usually needs a permit.

  • Live plants, seeds, soil and cuttings are tightly controlled for biosecurity.
  • Most require a phytosanitary certificate or are banned outright.
  • Declare on arrival without exception.
Import rules change often and vary by nationality. Confirm with the Croatia customs authority or your embassy before you travel.

Entering Croatia from a non-EU country, the European Commission says it is prohibited to bring plants, plant products and other objects in personal luggage unless a phytosanitary (plant health) certificate accompanies them: that includes plants to be planted, fruits, vegetables and cut flowers, and seeds for non-commercial use are only allowed with a phytosanitary certificate. The Commission lists one exception: no certificate is needed for fruits of pineapple, coconut, durian, banana or date. Between EU countries the rules cover plants and plant products grown in an EU country and free from pests or disease.

🌱 Plants and seeds: what the EU says for Croatia

Croatia applies the EU plant-health rules. These are the official statements.

❓ Quick answers

Can I bring seeds into Croatia?

From a non-EU country, seeds for non-commercial purposes are only allowed into the EU when accompanied by a phytosanitary certificate, according to the European Commission.

Can I bring plants into Croatia from outside the EU?

Not in your luggage unless a phytosanitary certificate accompanies them: the Commission says it is prohibited to introduce plants, plant products and other objects without one, including plants to be planted, fruits, vegetables and cut flowers.

Can I bring plants or seeds into Croatia from another EU country?

Your Europe covers plants and plant products, such as cut flowers, fruit or vegetables, as long as they have been grown in an EU country and are free from pests or disease; localised pest outbreaks can restrict the type and amount.

Do I need a certificate for fruit in Croatia?

From outside the EU, fruits of pineapple, coconut, durian, banana or date need no phytosanitary certificate. Other fruit does, although Your Europe also says you can bring a limited quantity of fruit and vegetables and does not state an amount.

What does EFSA advise travellers about plants?

EFSA advises that all plants and plant products, even cut flowers, must have a phytosanitary certificate to legally enter the EU, and that travellers leaving the EU should leave plants and plant products behind.
